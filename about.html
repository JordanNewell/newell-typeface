The score. Every glyph answers to it.

THE INTENT

Newell is a typeface that refuses curves. It started with a logo — a stylized N built from parallel rails and a decisive diagonal — and the mark suggested the whole alphabet. Not letters drawn one at a time, but one grammar extended: rails, diagonals, and the squared corners where they meet.

The result reads as engineered rather than drawn. Closer to a barcode or a circuit diagram than to a humanist serif. The signature gesture — that descending N diagonal — recurs in M, K, R, V, W, X, Y, Z, and the digits 1, 2, 4, 7. It's the family resemblance.

THE GRAMMAR

Three primitives. Nothing else.

RAILvertical / horizontal
RAILhorizontal bar
DIAGexactly 45 degrees

A rail runs horizontal or vertical. A diagonal runs at exactly 45 degrees — no exceptions. A junction is the squared corner where they meet. 

abs(dx) == abs(dy)

The generator asserts it. If a glyph cannot be expressed in rails + 45° diagonals, it does not belong in Newell.

FORBIDDEN

  • Curves of any kind (no Bézier control points)
  • Rounded terminals
  • Diagonals at angles other than 45 degrees
  • Outlines or metrics borrowed from existing typefaces

The full constitution lives in SPEC.md on GitHub.

THE S RULING

Newell's S has no curve to hide behind — it's Z's diagonal wearing S's name. Same 45° spine as the N, half-bars for the terminals. v0.2 buys it back with a step-diagonal. Until then, squint.

WHO

Jordan Newell — design and engineering. Built the parametric generator, wrote the SPEC, designed every glyph, compiled the binaries, made this page.

The font is original work generated from rules in SPEC.md. No existing typeface was traced or used as a metrics reference.
